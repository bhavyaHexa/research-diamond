# Hexa 3D Viewer (`/hexa-viewer.html`)

A general-purpose 3D model viewer on plain three.js with a readable frame pipeline. It does not import the vendored engine. The original engine viewer (`/`) is unchanged.

## Using it (`src/hexa-viewer/ui/`)

The page opens empty: a drop area plus a sample ring. Everything lives in one 440 px panel on the right (stacked under the model on a phone); the model area has nothing on top of it. Design: the approved Artifact "Hexa 3D Viewer UI"; palette and Roboto font are from hexacoder.com (`ui.css` variables).
- **Load a model:** drag files anywhere into the window, use Upload / Browse files, or paste a link (needs CORS). GLB and GLTF (with its `.bin` and textures dropped together), Draco and Meshopt compression (`public/draco/` holds the decoder, nothing is fetched from outside). Files are read in the browser only.
- **Fitting (`model.ts`):** an uploaded model is scaled so its longest side is 3 units (the sample ring's size), centred on the origin, and the ground goes to its lowest point. Camera range, shadow frustum and SSR distances were tuned on the ring and keep working for any model. The sample ring keeps its authored placement.
- **Materials:** *Jewelry mode* (diamond shader for stones named like the ring's `D1xxx / D4xxx / D5`, SSR metals) is on only for files that have those names; the Scene tab switch turns it off to use the file's own materials. For every other model, all PBR materials get SSR and SSAO (`enableSsr`).
- **Tabs:** Scene (materials grouped, show/hide, jewelry switch), Material (colour, roughness, metalness, Standard/Metal; sparkle for diamonds), Environment (built-in HDRs, upload `.hdr` / `.exr`, rotation, intensity, lock to camera, White / Clear / Colour background, ground shadow), Render (accumulated frames, resolution scale, exposure, contrast, saturation, bloom, SSR, AO). The footer has view modes (Beauty, Depth, Normals, AO from the pre-pass buffers), reset / fit / turntable / copy, the accumulation status and Export image (PNG, transparent when the background is Clear).
- **URL parameters:** `?sample` opens the sample ring; `?bare` hides the panel so the canvas fills the window (the compare scripts use `?sample&bare` to get the same framing as the engine viewer).
- **Code map:** `viewer.ts` (scene, loading, environment, capture), `model.ts` (file reading, fitting), `ui/ui.ts` (panel, plain DOM, sliders and switches in two small registries), `ui/ui.css`, `pipeline.ts` (view modes, `setBackground`, `applySettings`), `ground.ts` (baked shadow, `setHeight`, `setOpacity`). `scripts/compare/ui-smoke.mjs` drives the whole UI headless (upload, tabs, views, export, generic load, phone width).

## Glass material (`glass.ts`, `shaders/glass*.glsl`)

Select a material in the Scene tab, open the Material tab and choose **Glass**: every mesh that uses that material gets the glass shader; Standard or Metal restores the file's material. Controls: internal reflections, edge sharpness, refractive index, tint colour (default white) and strength, frosting, reflection, refraction depth. Made for hollow, thick-walled glass models such as a perfume bottle (the wall is modelled in the file); other parts (liquid, cap) keep their own materials, and a liquid mesh inside the glass is seen through it.
- **Bottle model (`public/assets/bottle-glass.glb`, made in Blender):** the default bottle is a clean, watertight, hollow glass solid built from the scanned `bottle.glb` (which is a dense, noisy outer surface with no wall, a closed neck cap and faceted shoulder). In Blender the outer surface was re-sampled in polar form (a ray from the axis per height and angle, 144 angles), smoothed (angle always, height only around the shoulder, so the threads stay sharp) and rebuilt as ONE manifold quad mesh: outer wall, foot ring and recessed bottom, flat lip, neck bore, funnel-shaped shoulder ceiling, rounded cavity floor (2 mm side wall, 1.6 mm roof, floor at 7 mm). 208k triangles, 0 non-manifold edges, smooth normals, cylindrical UVs (bottom and floor planar). The proportions already follow the reference photo (height x1.23, neck x1.3, taper 0.93 compared with the original). The original `bottle.glb` is kept untouched. The old run-time reshaping and wall generation (`reshape.ts`, `hollow.ts`) and the wall/base sliders were removed.
- **Default page:** `/hexa-viewer.html` loads `public/assets/bottle.glb` and applies glass to it (`?sample` loads the ring instead, `?empty` shows the empty state).
- **Real wall thickness:** comes from the model itself. The cavity surface is part of the same mesh (normals pointing into the cavity), so a view ray meets front 1, back 1 (cavity near side), front 2 (cavity far side), back 2 for the peeling layers. Default tint is white (clear).
- **Edges and outlines:** each surface the ray meets uses its own real normal, so bending near the edges comes from the shape. A ray bent far from the view direction sees the studio environment instead of the backdrop (dark and bright outlines), and the last interface adds its Fresnel reflection of the environment.
- **Matching the photo: light and material (`scripts/compare/tune-glass.mjs`, `glass-stats.mjs`, `montage.mjs`):** the photo is a soft, light glass, not a high-contrast chrome look. Brightness statistics inside the silhouette (photo: mean 213, darkest 5% at 106, median 232, 22.6% of pixels below 200, 5.4% pure white) are the score the tuner minimises while rendering several studio and material variants in one browser session; the montage tool puts photo and renders side by side for the judgement the numbers cannot make. Findings: (1) tone mapping decides everything: radiance 1 is the photo's light grey, radiance 3 saturates to pure white (74% of pixels at 250 or more), so the white tent is about 1.2 and the room behind the camera is dim (0.15), which is what the front surfaces mirror and why clear glass is a little darker than the backdrop; (2) internal softness (blurring the environment lookups inside the glass) removes the dark edge lines entirely, so it stays small (0.12); (3) the dark disc at the base was total internal reflection off the underside into the dim room, not the shadow (changing the shadow opacity changed nothing): a wide bright ceiling (`topStart` 0.2) turns it into the bright base ring of the photo; (4) thin walls (0.11, about 2 mm) narrow the side bands. Not matched yet: the glass interior is whiter than the photo (median 245 against 232) and there is no soft floor reflection.
- **Glass studio environment (`environments.ts`):** a generated equirect map (a dim room behind the camera, a bright tent behind the model, a lit floor, a wide overhead ceiling, soft darker bands at the sides, a key strip and a fill strip; every level is a field of `StudioLook`) offered as the first preset in the Environment tab and selected on the default bottle page. The page behind the model stays white; this only changes what the glass reflects and what strongly bent rays see. It is what gives glass on white its dark and bright outlines. Rotation and intensity sliders apply as for any HDR.
- **Internal reflections and edge lines:** `Internal reflections` (default 2) adds a second internal bounce (off the far wall, then the front wall's inner side), weighted by both Fresnel terms so it only shows along the contour and the edges. `Edge sharpness` (default 0.6) finds bevels and facet edges from how fast the view-space normal changes per pixel (`fwidth(N)`) and lets those pixels catch the studio as a crisp opaque line; it is faded out at grazing angles because the silhouette always turns the normal fast. This replaced the planned "faceted normals" switch: on a 216k-triangle mesh flat shading is just noise, while the crease detector marks exactly the bevels. Both start small on purpose (a first version at full weight painted a white halo round the silhouette).
- **Shadow and caustics (`caustics.ts`, `shaders/photon.*`, `lightview.*`):** the ground bake keeps the shadow in the green channel and caustic light in the red channel; the ground material shows `shadow - light`. With `Caustics` above 0 (Environment tab, default 1) glass casts a full shadow and, for each of the 64 light samples, a regular grid of photons (320 x 320, offset differently every sample) is cast from the light through the glass: they enter where the light-view position and map say and pass straight through (the glass is treated as a thin plate; the heavy base's lens effect was removed with the wall/base code), each is tinted by the glass absorption and weakened by Fresnel, and it is splatted where it lands. Their energy is normalised so unobstructed light adds up to 1 per ground texel, so clear glass lifts most of its own shadow. With `Caustics` at 0 glass casts a fixed lighter shadow (`SETTINGS.groundShadow.glass`, 0.5) instead. Adds about 0.2 s to the bake; the bake is redone when a glass slider is released. Limitation: photons ignore other objects, so a liquid mesh inside the glass would have its shadow lifted too.
- **Why not three.js transmission:** it is one screen-space layer with a constant thickness, and it would see our alpha-0 clear as a half-transparent white backdrop.
- **Frame:** glass meshes  are hidden from the normal colour pass and from the depth pre-pass (SSR and AO ignore them). When any exist: (1) the scene without glass is copied to a `refraction` target; (2) four layers of the glass are found by **depth peeling** (front 1, back 1, front 2 skipping front 1, back 2 skipping back 1; half-float, depth stored relative to the glass's distance from the camera to stay precise); (3) the glass meshes are drawn into the scene target, depth-tested against the opaque scene.
- **Shader (`glass.chunk.glsl`):** reflection from the material's own lighting (environment and lights, Fresnel from the IOR, black diffuse). The ray refracts in at front 1, crosses the wall, leaves at back 1 (the cavity's near side, or the outer far side where there is no cavity); across the cavity it travels the air gap, enters the far wall at front 2 and leaves at back 2. Each hop uses that surface's normal and the path length measured between the layers; the screen position where the ray lands picks the colour from `refraction`; Beer-Lambert absorption tints it by the glass path; total internal reflection shows the environment. Output is premultiplied (`alpha` = how much of the page stays hidden) so it composites over the white page and over a Clear background.
- **Limits:** the layer walk assumes a cavity with one wall on each side (a hollow inside a hollow, or a liquid mesh in the cavity, is seen but not refracted as a separate medium); no roughness blur of the refraction (frosting only blurs the reflection); the caustic light is greyscale; the liquid and cap have no special shaders yet; the caustic photons use a thin-plate model; four peel layers cost four extra passes over the glass geometry.
- **Testing:** `src/hexa-viewer/dev/testBottle.ts` builds a perfume bottle (thick shell with a cavity, liquid, no cap) and is available as `window.hexaDev.makeTestBottle()` in the dev server only; `scripts/compare/glass-smoke.mjs` drops it onto the page, applies Glass and checks the result.

## One frame (`src/hexa-viewer/pipeline.ts`)

1. **Depth/normal pre-pass** (`ssr.ts`, `shaders/depth.*`): view-space distance and normal of everything drawn, jittered like the colour frame.
2. **SSAO** (`ssao.ts`, `shaders/ssao*.glsl`): 11-sample spiral occlusion plus a bilateral blur. The metal multiplies its indirect light by it.
3. **Scene colour**: three.js draws the ring into a half-float target **that has a depth buffer**. The gold is `MeshPhysicalMaterial` patched with SSR; the stones use the diamond shader in `src/render/`; the ground only receives the shadow.
4. **Accumulate** (`shaders/accumulate.frag.glsl`): running average of 32 jittered frames (anti-aliasing, noise removal), in full-float targets. Each frame is clamped to 16, like the engine's RGBM range.
5. **Bloom** (`shaders/bloom.frag.glsl`): prefilter at threshold 2, five down/up levels.
6. **Composite** (`shaders/composite.frag.glsl`): ACES, contrast 1.12, saturation 1, sRGB, white background where nothing is drawn.

All numbers are in `src/hexa-viewer/settings.ts`.

## Lights (`src/hexa-viewer/ground.ts`)

Two directional lights, because the shipped scene lights the gold from a low side direction `(-0.9, 0.5, -1)` but its soft ground shadow falls from almost straight above:
- `light` lights the metal (colour 0xCCE5FF, intensity 1). It casts no shadow.
- `shadowLight` (intensity ~0) casts only the ground shadow, from about `(0.01, 1, 0.01)`.
- **The soft shadow is baked once in world space** (`ground.bake`, called from `viewer.ts`): the light is moved by `spread` for each of 64 shadow maps, each map is added into one 1024x1024 texture seen from straight above, and the ground shows that texture (black with the texture as alpha). The shadow therefore does not depend on the camera or on the frame accumulation: it is complete, soft and in place from the first frame after the camera moves. (It used to be jittered per frame and averaged by the accumulation, so after every camera move it restarted as one hard, off-centre shadow that drifted to the centre over 32 frames.) `shadowLight` only casts while baking. `ground.setDirection` rebakes; `scripts/compare/shadow-sync.mjs` and `shadow-bake-stats.mjs` check it.

## Things worth knowing

- **The scene render target needs a depth buffer.** It was missing at first. Without it, hidden surfaces overwrite visible ones and the gold looked like streaked, blocky noise. This was the main reason the gold did not match; fixing it dropped the per-cell gold error from about 26-40 levels to about 14-24 and made the pattern match.
- The engine's camera-fixed environment keeps reflection directions in view space and rotates them only about Y. The environment is built as `camera rotation * Y rotation(2)` (a matrix, not an Euler-component add) in `viewer.ts`; the shipped yaw is +2.
- Diamond stones include the band stones `D1xxx`, halo `D4xxx` and centre `D5`.
- Stone brightness: the stored diamond `envMapIntensity` is 1.6, but fitting the stones' average against the shipped frame gives about 1.4 (`SETTINGS.stones.envIntensity`). The reason is not known.

## Checking against the engine (headless, no preview pane)

```bash
npm run dev            # dev server on :5173
node scripts/compare/compare-own-vs-engine.mjs "base=" "ssr15=ssr.ssrIntensity.value=1.5"
node scripts/compare/capture-frames.mjs        # writes tmp-engine.png and tmp-own.png
```

Both need Chrome at `C:/Program Files/Google/Chrome/Application/chrome.exe` and `puppeteer-core` (installed in `node_modules` but not listed in `package.json`; add it as a dev dependency). The compare script renders the engine once at the end-of-intro camera, renders the own viewer once per setup (each argument is `name=js`, run in the own page with `own`, `ssr` and `window.settings` available) and prints, per class of grid cells (gold, stone, shadow, all), the mean difference (own - engine) and the mean absolute difference. Cells are classified from the engine's colour.

## How the gold was matched (2026-11-13)

The method was to split the problem instead of tuning numbers (scripts in `scripts/compare/`):
1. `compare-post.mjs`: the engine's own HDR frame through our composite pass reproduces the engine's final image (mean abs 1.2 levels), so tone mapping, contrast and sRGB are correct.
2. `compare-shading.mjs`: with SSR off in both viewers, the gold matches within 2-3% (correlation 0.98), so the material, HDR environment, light and AO are correct. Only SSR differed.
3. `compare-depth.mjs`: the engine's G-buffer depth and ours agree (ground included), so the SSR inputs match.
4. `compare-ssr-history.mjs`: giving our SSR the engine's history frame did not change the result, so the difference was in the shader logic.
5. Reading the engine's compiled gold shader (`engine-gold-shader.mjs`) showed two things we had wrong: the front-ray mask has the opposite sign (`-4 * dot(viewDir, normal) + 3.8`), and a hit is accepted even when it lands slightly before the ray start (t < 0, i.e. a ray skimming its own surface hits it). The second was the main cause: it makes the engine's SSR darken the gold about twice as much at grazing angles.
Other details copied from the engine: GGX-sampled reflection direction with interleaved gradient noise, depth looked up bilinearly as sqrt(normalised depth), and the engine settles over several accumulation cycles (so measure it after at least 4-5 cycles).

## How the stones were matched

Same split method (`scripts/compare/`): the diamond shader text, uniforms, gem environment texture (512x512 half float) and stone geometry were all identical to the engine's, yet the stones' pixel correlation was only about 0.5. The cause was found by reading the engine's per-stone data:
- **The engine shares one normal-capture cube between many stones.** The cube captured from `D4001` is used by every halo stone and by the centre stone `D5`; the cube from `D1001` by all band stones. Each stone still uses its own frame (centre, alignment matrix) in the shader. We captured one cube per stone. Sharing per group (`src/render/threeMaterials.ts`, `whitenDiamonds`) raised the stone correlation from about 0.5 to 0.85 and halved the stone error. (`find-capture-source.mjs` identified D4001 by capturing every stone and correlating with the engine's cube.)
- The gem environment is used as the raw equirect texture (the engine compiles the diamond program with `ENV_MAP_TYPE 1`), and the normal cube is read with nearest filtering (`src/render/threeLights.ts`, `diamond.ts`).
- Bloom now follows the engine: per-level weights from radius 0.15 and power 7.94, prefilter threshold 2 with soft threshold 0.03, and a final 4-tap blur of the glow (`pipeline.ts`, `bloom.frag.glsl`).
- Engine and own stone frames differ by axis signs (ours negate x, sometimes y); that does not matter as long as each group's frames are consistent.

## Known differences (as measured, after the stone fix)

- Whole frame: mean absolute cell difference 1.25 of 255.
- Gold: mean difference (+2.9, +4.8, +5.8), abs 5.0.
- Stones: mean difference about 0 at `envIntensity` 1.4, abs 8.4 (was 17). The stored value is 1.6; 1.4 is a fit and the reason for the 12% gap is not known (the stone correlation is now 0.85).
- Shadow: mean difference about -2.5, abs 3.6.
- No depth of field, SSGI, bevel, contact shadows or other optional effects: the shipped scene has them off.

### Product-shot look (floor reflection, studio, camera)
- **Floor reflection:** `applyGlass` adds a mirrored copy of every glass mesh under the ground (`userData.reflection`, a glass material that shares the real one's uniforms plus `glassFade`). The glass pass draws it like any glass; the shader multiplies it by `strength * exp(-depth below the floor / length)` (`SETTINGS.floorReflection`, 0.09 and 1.2). The ground planes no longer write depth, so the copy shows through; the bake ignores the copies. It is a crisp mirror image that fades, not a blurred one.
- **Studio for an elevated camera:** with the camera 18 degrees above the bottle the front surfaces mirror the overhead light, which whitened the whole body. The overhead light now starts at elevation 0.6, room 0.08, side flags 0.03 (soft 0.2), strips 8 (`DEFAULT_STUDIO`): dark outlines and a dark base disc like the photo (darkest 5% at 51-61 against 106, share below 120 about 9-11% against 7%). The default page camera is (0, 3.2, 10.2).
- Not matched yet: body interior still a little too white (median 244 against 232), the reflection is not blurred, the shoulder corner is a little less rolled than the photo's.

- **Neck flare and shoulder (Blender):** the outer profile was rebuilt per angle as a straight side line and a flatter straight roof line (from the neck at 4 mm to the side line at 9 mm), joined with a 3 mm corner fillet and a 4.5 mm concave flare into the neck (smooth min/max); `bottle-glass.glb` was re-exported (same 208k triangles, 0 non-manifold edges).
